The Garden of Consequences

An interactive adaptation of Hieronymus Bosch's The Garden of Earthly Delights, c.1500 — a major early Netherlandish work now in the Prado Museum, Madrid. Point and pinch to uncover another layer of its world. The brief: artwork, interaction and controlled generation.

Open the experience · Listen to narration and read the research · 19-second overview

Reveal is the default and the main story. The four time studies are optional experiences within the same prototype.

On a webpage

  1. Refresh the page after this update. The Mode menu stays at the top left.
  2. Choose Reveal, Garden Day / Night, Aftermath Day / Night, Garden Seasons, or Aftermath Seasons. Mode changes keep your viewing direction.
  3. Drag the image to look around. In Reveal, click to open or close a window onto the other world.
  4. Select Enable sound. Voice, captions and volume have separate controls. Replay voice repeats the current scene’s narration; Research story plays the research reading.

In Quest or another WebXR headset

  1. Open this same public HTTPS page in the headset browser. Choose Enter VR.
  2. A floating menu appears in front of you. Point a controller or hand ray at an option, then press the trigger or pinch to select it.
  3. In Reveal, hold pinch or trigger to reveal the other world; release to close it.
  4. To change experiences, point at the small MODES button in the lower part of your view, then pinch or press the trigger. Controller grip also reopens the menu; A/X may open it on supported controllers.
  5. The menu includes Enable sound / Mute sound, Replay narration, Research story, and Close menu.

When the menu is open, trigger and pinch select menu items. Close it or select an experience to return to Reveal interaction. The recorded environment has no automatic camera pan, rotation or zoom; turning your own head changes where you look.

The research enters the story

The opening narration introduces Bosch’s apparently abundant garden. The first reveal asks whether the other world is a punishment or a consequence. The garden’s day/night narration names art historian Margaret D. Carroll and her reading of time and transformation. The seasonal aftermath narration explicitly identifies recovery as our reinterpretation.

Carroll attends to world history, changing light, water and flame, and motifs transformed between panels. Her account is a scholarly interpretation, rather than a proven statement of Bosch’s intention. Carroll, Yale University Press, 2022.

The original work is a triptych, with Eden, earthly pleasures and Hell. The Prado tradition interprets the central garden through desire and fragile earthly happiness. Our aligned worlds, the need for rest and the possibility of recovery are contemporary extensions. American Friends of the Prado.

Production and audio

These optional modes are short authored hybrid loops. They reuse existing Orbis captures and controlled object animation, with fixed structural composition, authored light/weather changes, and selected image-generated references. The default experience and time studies play prepared media. The separate Live Lab requests actual model frames and uses authored masks and sky-driven lighting.

English narration uses Lily, an ElevenLabs stock voice, with eight scene-aware cues. The Suno score uses the user’s exported recording; environmental beds are authored sound design. Narration runs once per scene encounter and can be replayed. Desktop checks include loading, video loops, menu controls and sound; physical headset comfort and hand targeting require the wearer’s review.
