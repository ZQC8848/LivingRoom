Two styles. Two applications.

Project brief: an interactive adaptation of Bosch. The painting supplies the narrative; authored layouts and masks control how viewers reveal it. Live generation is an optional layer.

Try the main story first

  1. Open the stable experience. Reveal is the default.
  2. In VR, hold pinch or trigger to reveal the other world; release to close it.
  3. Use Mode, or the in-world MODES menu, for the four optional day/night and seasonal studies. Those studies play prepared video.
  4. Enable sound to hear English scene narration. Research story plays the attributed interpretation behind the painted experience.

Try actual live generation

  1. Open Live Lab before entering VR. On the demo computer, use the private operator launch page; on Quest, enter the private operator code.
  2. Choose Garden or Aftermath, then Start Live. Wait for the generated chunks and video frames. Nothing is billed while this page is idle.
  3. Choose Dusk, Winter, Rain or Rest / Day, or type a short weather request. These send prompts into the current model session. They do not select recorded videos.
  4. Look above the buildings at the open sky and down at the foreground. Buildings and figures keep their shapes on the approved plate. Their light can follow the received sky frames; the Follow sky light control compares that authored relighting.
  5. In VR, point at an option and pinch or press the trigger. LIVE MENU below your view, or the controller grip, reopens the panel.
  6. Stop Live releases the owned model session. Each connection has a three-minute cap. Exit VR and use Return to stable Reveal to resume the main story.

Read the status correctly

NOT LIVE means idle, connecting, priming or stopped. LIVE means generated chunks and received video frames have been observed. Prompt accepted confirms the model received a request. Fresh generated chunk confirms later output; the viewer still judges whether the requested change is visible. Rain and snow detail remain experimental and can be stylized.

How the painted world stays recognizable

The video model generates environmental sky and foreground material. A compositing mask limits where that stream appears over the approved painting. The geometry, layout and outlines of the architecture, figures, animals and river remain protected. Scene brightness, tint and broad soft cloud shadows are estimated from actual received video pixels, rather than from the prompt text. This is authored compositing, not a physical simulation or a fully generated painting. This is a bounded hybrid use of the real-time model. No automatic camera transform or generated camera path is applied to the painting.

The environmental reference has a 2:1 content region contained inside the model frame with measured margins. Those margins are removed when mapping received frames to the sphere. The stable Reveal and prepared studies remain independent of the live connection.

Live requires available sponsor capacity, the operator's computer and the temporary HTTPS tunnel. A private code protects model-session creation; the public page contains no Reactor API key.

Research belongs to the painting

Margaret D. Carroll interprets Bosch through time, material transformation and the changing life of the world. That attributed reading informs the garden's narration. Recovery and rest are contemporary interpretive questions in this project. The realistic sheep study is presented on its own technical and visual merits.
