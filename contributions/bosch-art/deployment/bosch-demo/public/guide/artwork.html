Hieronymus Bosch, The Garden of Earthly Delights, c.1500.
A major work of early Netherlandish painting in the Northern Renaissance, now in the Prado Museum, Madrid.

Use your hand to uncover another reading of the same world.

We reconstruct the painting as aligned panoramic layers. Point and pinch to reveal the darker layer; release to return. The interaction makes the relationship between the images something you discover for yourself.

Designed layers. Bounded generation.

Creators design the composition, masks and correspondence between views. Optional real-time video adds weather in selected sky and foreground regions; its light also informs authored scene relighting. The painting's main forms stay under artistic control.

A method to explore with other paintings

We are testing a repeatable approach: panoramic layout, gesture-driven image layers and controlled generative elements. Each new artwork needs its own meaningful relationship between layers and its own masks. Bosch is our current case study.

Prepared demo: Reveal and four short time-study loops. Live Lab: an experimental real model stream, subject to capacity and variable visual results.

Artwork details, sources and adaptation credits

Original artist: Hieronymus Bosch (c.1450-1516). Oil on oak panels. Dating: 1490-1500 according to the Prado/CODART; the visitor brief uses c.1500. Original held at the Museo Nacional del Prado, Madrid, on permanent loan from Patrimonio Nacional.

Interpretations remain debated. A common reading connects earthly delight with its fragility and darker consequences. Our hand reveal, aligned layouts, seasons and weather are contemporary adaptations. The panorama simplifies and rearranges Bosch's motifs for interaction; it is not an exact scan of the original panels.

Sources: CODART / Prado work record; Prado picture history; National Gallery artist biography; Margaret D. Carroll on time and transformation. Reproduction: Wikimedia Commons. Immersive adaptation: Living Room team.
