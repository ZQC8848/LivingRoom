The Garden of Consequences

What happens when a garden is never allowed to rest?

A painted garden promises abundance. As daylight fades, its familiar structures remain, but their meaning changes. Darkness offers an interval in which the viewer can look again. The experience connects the desire to keep a beautiful moment with the need to let a world change.

Research and interpretation

Bosch’s Garden of Earthly Delights is a triptych: Eden on the left, earthly pleasures in the centre, and Hell on the right. The Prado’s catalogue reads the central garden as a deceptive paradise of lust. The project’s aligned garden and aftermath are contemporary reinterpretations, rather than two original paintings or a literal reconstruction. American Friends of the Prado; Prado catalogue.

Art historian Margaret D. Carroll proposes a reading centred on time and transformation: the world’s generation and decline, changing light, water and flame, and motifs transformed between panels. This provides a research basis for treating the environment as a process. It is an attributed scholarly interpretation, not a settled account of Bosch’s intention. Carroll, Yale University Press, 2022.

The need for rest is this project’s own interpretive extension. Night is not equated with sin, and daytime is not proof of innocence. Likewise, possible recovery in the aftermath is an artistic proposition; the original Hell should not be confused with Catholic Purgatory. Catholic Catechism on Purgatory.

Two forms of agency

Reveal lets the viewer inspect the relationship between abundance and its possible consequences. The existing public Reveal experience remains unchanged.

Time proposes a different decision: when to let the light fade, how long to remain, and when to return. In a future on-request version, the viewer would select “Let night fall” or “Bring back the day.” These controls should belong to a separate Time mode so they do not compete with the pinch used for Reveal. Painting interaction is deferred.

Pacing

0–3 seconds: daylight. 3–12: light fades. 12–18: night holds. 18–27: dawn returns. 27–30: daylight. The water variation is cyclic; the original viewpoint and panorama mapping remain fixed.

English voice over

Original interpretive writing, not a quotation from Bosch or Carroll. Narration should play once per encounter rather than repeat on every ambient cycle.

Everything seems to be in bloom. As though this moment could last forever.
Let the light fade. Look at what remains.
Night is not the garden’s punishment. Even abundance needs a pause.
